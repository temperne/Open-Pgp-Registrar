Protect your home from deed fraud, for free

Register a digital signature key with the County Recorder once. From then on, a deed against your property has to carry your signature, which only you can make. A forged deed without it is held for investigation instead of being recorded.

The problem

In deed fraud, someone forges a property owner's signature, often with a forged notary stamp too, and records a deed that "transfers" the property. Then they sell it or borrow against it. The Recorder's office accepts documents that look right; it has no way to tell a forged signature from a real one.

Once a forged deed is recorded, the transfer can't simply be undone. The real owner has to go to court and bring a quiet title action, which is slow and expensive. Owners often don't find out until long after. Vacant land, rental properties, and homes of older or absent owners are common targets.

Why this program exists

Digital signatures solved this problem for software and email decades ago. A digital signature can only be made by the person who holds the private key, and anyone can check it with free software. S.T.O.P. brings that same protection to the paper deeds the county already records, without changing the county's recording software and without extra cost to owners.

How it works

  1. Create a key. This website makes a key pair in your browser. You keep the private key (a PDF you print and save on a USB drive, locked with your passphrase). Nothing is sent anywhere.
  2. Register it, free. Print the registration form. Sign it before a notary, and have a local law enforcement officer confirm your identity. Before activating the key, the Recorder calls the officer's agency to confirm. A forger would have to fake the stamp and fool a live call.
  3. Sign your documents. When you sell or transfer the property, the deed is written here and signed with your key. It prints with QR codes that carry your signature and the exact words you signed. You still sign in ink before a notary, as always; the digital signature is an extra layer.
  4. The Recorder checks it. Counter staff scan the codes (with a handheld scanner, a phone, or a scan of the document). The tool confirms the signature matches the key on file and shows the exact signed text to compare with the paper.
  5. Forgeries are held. A deed on a registered parcel with no signature, the wrong signature, or altered text is held and investigated, and the owner is notified.

How it solves the problem

  • Stops forgeries before title moves. A forger can copy a signature or a notary stamp, but can't make your digital signature without your private key and passphrase.
  • Catches altered documents. The signature covers every word and number. Change the buyer's name, the price or the legal description, and the check fails.
  • Helps in court. If a forged deed ever slipped through, the record would show that the required signature was missing, so a quiet title action could be faster and cheaper.
  • Costs almost nothing. No new county software. The county needs a short staff training, one new form, and a scanner or phone at the counter. The software is free and open source.
  • Voluntary. Owners who don't enroll see no change at all.
  • Private. Only your public key is filed. It can check signatures but cannot make them. This website stores nothing and sends nothing.

Questions

Do I still need a notary and an ink signature?

Yes. Everything about recording stays the same. The digital signature is added on top.

What if I lose my private key or forget my passphrase?

Register a new key with the same identity checks (notary, officer and callback). The old key is retired. Keep a printed copy of your key PDF in a safe place, and the passphrase somewhere separate.

Could someone copy the QR codes from one of my real deeds onto a forged one?

No. The codes carry the exact text you signed. Staff see that text when they scan, so it won't match a different deed.

Is my key or my document sent anywhere?

No. This website runs entirely in your browser and is blocked from making network connections. You can even save it and use it offline.

We own the property together, or through a trust or company. How does that work?

It's your choice. One key can cover everyone: name it with a list of the owners or the entity's name. Or register several keys, for example the trust and its beneficiary, or each spouse, and describe each key however you like. Each key holder files their own registration form for each parcel, listing every key (swap public key cards to do this), and one key can be registered on any number of parcels. Every listed key must then sign a deed: together on one computer, or one after another by passing the partly signed PDF along ("Sign exactly as others"). A person at the Recorder judges whether the structure fits the deed.

What if I don't sign up?

Nothing changes for you. The program is voluntary.

What technology does it use?

OpenPGP, an open standard for digital signatures used for decades to protect software and email. Every signature can also be checked with free tools such as GnuPG.
